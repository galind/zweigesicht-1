## Visual diagnosis

The saved browser close-up has broad uniform gray bridge fields, bright almost-white continuous edges, very dark blue screw heads, opaque red jewel rings, and largely uniform gold barrels. The maker image has much more directional bridge reflectance, fine face graining, sharper dark/light transitions at polished edges, brighter saturated blue head highlights, and pink/purple jewel interiors. The maker image also includes a large blue display ring absent from the browser view; compare corresponding components rather than treating the images as matching configurations. The saved image alone cannot prove temporal aliasing or distinguish all normal defects from lighting effects.

The current material shader expresses grain through base-color modulation and scalar roughness/height, while all materials remain isotropic `MeshStandardMaterial`. This can draw stripes without producing the directional reflection that makes brushed metal convincing. The physical response should carry the finish at normal viewing distances; microscopic structure should resolve only at sufficiently close views.

## Concrete defects and priorities

1. **Broad face versus everything else is not face versus bevel.** `roughnessFactor=mix(.10,roughnessFactor,finishFace)` makes walls, tooth flanks, recess walls and engravings polished whenever they are not nearly axial faces. This plausibly contributes to the uniform white outlines in the saved close-up. Use separate face, bevel and wall masks. A normal-angle bevel mask is only a heuristic: curved walls pass through intermediate angles too. Prefer source-face classification where the CAD audit can supply it, or combine orientation with known axial-height bands and exact definition assignments. Never claim this creates or verifies bevel geometry.
2. **Classification uses an unnormalized interpolated local normal.** Normalize `vFinishNormal` before its dot product with the finish axis. Its interpolated length can be below one on curved surfaces, changing the apparent material class even when orientation is otherwise similar. Preserve the source normal attribute; correct material classification without blanket welding or smoothing.
3. **The texture has large, partly unfiltered structure.** Straight noise at 12 cells/mm has an approximately 83 micrometre cell spacing; the added 5/mm variation is approximately 200 micrometres. Circular `sin(r*113.097)` has an approximately 56 micrometre repeat and perfectly coherent rings. Additional bridge, circular and frosted noise terms lack footprint filtering. These are visible decoration scales, not a substitute for unresolved microfacet statistics. Base-color variation of up to several percent reinforces a stained/woodgrain appearance.
4. **The circular origin and face axis are inferred from bounding boxes.** Bounding-box center can shift away from a wheel's rotational center due to an eccentric feature; smallest box extent is not a reliable component frame for tilted imported parts. Add exact definition-local finish frames (`originMm`, plane axes, normal axis) for high-visibility families, validated against source axes/bore centers and stored separately from generated geometry. Leave a documented fallback for unreviewed catalog parts.
5. **Blued steel is currently an ordinary dark colored conductor.** `0x174e87`, metalness 1 and a room reflection can yield almost black heads. Raising global exposure would also wash out steel edges. Tune the reflection rig and the blue material together, using a recognizable dark-to-blue transition and a restrained bright highlight. A generic strong clearcoat or rainbow film is not automatically an improvement.
6. **Superseded material interpretation:** the earlier review treated definition 155 as a separate red optical mass based on the exploded-render color. The user's subsequent physical-material correction identifies it as steel, consistent with the gray CAD appearance. It now uses neutral polished steel and no jewel transmission; the red gauge inlays remain separate.

## Exact anisotropy integration in installed Three r186

Use `MeshPhysicalMaterial` for brushed/circular families. In `src/renderers/webgl/WebGLPrograms.js`, `material.anisotropy > 0` enables `USE_ANISOTROPY`; `MeshPhysicalMaterial` handles its uniform and program-version transition. Setting an arbitrary uniform on `MeshStandardMaterial` does not enable this path.

The exporter in `scripts/cad/export_assembly.py::GLB.mesh` writes only `POSITION` and `NORMAL`, without UV or tangent attributes. The installed `normal_fragment_begin.glsl.js` builds `tbn` from supplied tangents, or from `getTangentFrame(-vViewPosition,normal,vUv)`. `normalmap_pars_fragment.glsl.js` returns zero tangent/bitangent for constant UV derivatives. Thus simply setting `anisotropy=.5` on these CAD meshes does not provide a valid anisotropic frame.

A geometry-preserving integration is:

1. Retain `vFinishPosition=position` and local normals. Pass the two chosen local plane directions transformed into view space from the vertex stage: `mat3(modelViewMatrix)*finishAxisU` and `mat3(modelViewMatrix)*finishAxisV`. These are directions, so translation must not apply. The current source placements/motion are rigid; if later nonuniform scaling is allowed, revalidate frame construction rather than treating the normal matrix as a universal direction transform.
2. After `#include <normal_fragment_maps>`, project the intended view-space broad-lobe direction onto the final shading normal plane. Normalize it with a nonzero-length guard, then construct the orthogonal companion with `cross(normal,T)`. Select a stable alternate direction on near-parallel walls. Use this to replace the relevant `tbn` columns, or replace the `tbn` initializer in the installed normal chunk. This avoids adding UVs or tangent attributes to shared source buffers. Re-orthogonalizing after any small normal perturbation also prevents a stale tangent frame.
3. After `#include <lights_physical_fragment>` and before `#include <lights_fragment_begin>`, set the final `material.anisotropy`, `material.anisotropyT`, `material.anisotropyB` and `material.alphaT`. Recompute `alphaT = mix(pow2(material.roughness),1.0,pow2(material.anisotropy))` whenever masking the strength. Preserve the installed chunk's geometric roughness correction and physical-material initialization rather than replacing the complete lighting chunk.
4. Gate anisotropy with the existing `finishEnabled` uniform and face mask. Leave the material's positive anisotropy property stable so Function/selection/context changes do not cause repeated shader recompilation. The fragment strength becomes zero in those modes. Other new optical effects need equivalent restoration in `MovementViewer.retargetVisibility`; it currently restores only color/metalness/roughness and the procedural enabled flag.
5. Give the new shader source a new `customProgramCacheKey`, retain per-material uniforms, assert replacement anchors exist, and actually compile all families in a WebGL browser. Existing CPU hook assertions do not prove GLSL compilation.

**Direction convention matters.** `lights_physical_fragment.glsl.js` explicitly makes tangent roughness increase with anisotropy; bitangent roughness remains the material roughness. For a surface with low slope along scratches and greater slope across them, assign the broad-lobe tangent across the scratches. For circular machining this is radial, with the narrow-lobe bitangent tangential. In local plane coordinates `(u,v)`, a radial candidate is `u*axisU+v*axisV`. Normalize with a radius guard and fade anisotropy in a tiny central region rather than allowing a singularity. For straight brushing choose the cross-grain axis explicitly. Visually verify the resulting reflection orientation; do not infer it from a variable named `tangent` alone.

The installed `lights_fragment_maps.glsl.js` calls `getIBLAnisotropyRadiance` using `material.anisotropyB`. `envmap_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js` implements a bent-normal PMREM approximation, not a fully anisotropically convolved environment. Direct lighting uses the anisotropic GGX path in `lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js`. These are useful built-in responses, with an honest IBL approximation limit.

## Physically scaled, filtered surface detail

Keep all procedural coordinates in component-local millimetres. Parameterize detail with explicit spacing in mm and perturbation amplitude in mm, not object-relative repeat counts. A reasonable **authored tuning trial**, not a measured finish claim, is 5–20 micrometre fine detail with near-zero base-color modulation, most of its appearance supplied by roughness and anisotropy. Do not multiply the present frequencies while retaining the same height: at fixed height, higher frequency increases surface slope and can make the bump worse.

Filter every octave using the maximum derivative footprint of its scaled coordinate. Fade a band's amplitude continuously to zero when its footprint becomes unresolved; preserve a stable mean roughness/anisotropy for the missing microstructure. Avoid a high-frequency unfiltered secondary layer after filtering the primary. Avoid perfectly coherent sine rings as the main barrel treatment. If larger manufacturing variation is retained, make it very weak and reference-specific.

For normal perturbation, the current derivative construction follows a surface-gradient form and does not displace geometry, but its amplitude must shrink with frequency. Begin with no bump; add a subtle slope only if matched macro views improve. A procedural frosted plate should resolve as many tiny irregular reflections and a soft mean response, not broad color mottling. Use isotropic high-frequency roughness variation with filtering; do not impose bridge anisotropy on it.

## Reflection rig, blue and jewel tuning

`MovementViewer` currently makes PMREM from the full default `RoomEnvironment` with blur sigma .04, intensity .9, plus a hemisphere light and two directional lights. The installed room includes six boxes, room walls and six bright emissive panels; its reflections are generic room structure, not a maker-matched macro studio rig. Replace it with a small authored PMREM scene containing broad key/fill cards, a narrower edge card and dark space between them. Preserve enough fill on both movement and dial sides. Tune cards and roughness first with bump disabled. Reducing preprocessing blur cautiously can preserve polished reflection detail; validate for aliasing rather than assuming zero blur is best.

Centralize environment generation and reuse it in both construction and `onContextRestored`; currently these have duplicated room setup. Dispose old PMREM targets and temporary geometry/materials exactly as the existing lifecycle does. Keep the environment world-fixed during user orbit so highlights describe form, while testing all camera sides for readability. Avoid baking assembly contact into the reflection environment.

For blue metal, first tune a less crushed blue conductor with the new reflections. A restrained `MeshPhysicalMaterial` film treatment is an optional authored approximation; the installed chunk supports `iridescence`, `iridescenceIOR` and thickness, but none of those values is measured by the reference image. Do not add emission to rescue darkness. Separate screw-head finish from shafts only with a reviewed local frame/height rule or source-face evidence.

For jewels, use a dielectric base and neutral specular response. `MeshPhysicalMaterial` supports IOR and transmission with attenuation, but transmission needs sensible thickness in this scene's millimetre units, adds render cost, and is not necessary to get a strong first improvement. Test opaque optical approximation first, then compare modest transmission on a representative exposed jewel if it materially helps. Preserve opacity 1 when using Three's physical transmission. Recheck jewels from the back and near reflective chatons; a single frontal screenshot can hide sorting/refraction limitations. Give the red shock mass its own family before changing jewel optics globally.

## Contact shading

The current unit normalization is appropriate: installed SSAO uses normalized linear-depth differences, so dividing the mm thresholds by `(far-near)` is consistent. Radius .8 is in view-space mm. The present strength is .48.

However, the beauty shader has already applied tone mapping and output colorspace conversion when `SurfaceOcclusion.render` multiplies the default framebuffer. Installed `SSAOPass.copyMaterial` uses `DstColorFactor/ZeroFactor`, and `CopyShader` simply returns sampled AO. Consequently this overlay darkens the entire displayed result, including direct and polished specular highlights, rather than only indirect light. It is a visual approximation and can exaggerate dark outlines. Its small box blur is not a depth-aware denoiser.

Low-risk iteration: lower strength to an authored .2–.3 trial, reduce world radius for smaller contacts, and compare assembled/separated macros with AO toggled. If the extra pass is retained, qualify the final settings empirically. More substantive improvement: generate AO before beauty, pass its screen-space texture to materials, and inject indirect-only modulation beside `aomap_fragment`. The installed AO chunk already shows the intended operations: attenuate `reflectedLight.indirectDiffuse` and use `computeSpecularOcclusion(dotNV,ambientOcclusion,material.roughness)` on `indirectSpecular`; preserve direct terms. That requires restructuring pass order/targets, avoiding feedback, and redoing lifecycle and context-recovery checks. It is an implementation choice, not a requirement to introduce a larger composer.
