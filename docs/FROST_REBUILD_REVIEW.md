# Frosted metal replacement — 11 September 2026

This is a new appearance proposal, not user acceptance. Baseline: `9482cc7`. The previous color/roughness noise and the previous prohibition on frost normal detail are superseded. No source or derived CAD, reference media, or site has been uploaded or published.

## Reference basis and visual diagnosis

Visually reviewed the original maker [Front 2 Werk photograph](https://www.marcolangwatches.com/en/downloads-2/) and SJX's [Zweigesicht-1 review](https://watchesbysjx.com/2023/08/marco-lang-zweigesicht-1-review.html), particularly movement detail 1 and detail 5. Their local files match the SHA-256 records `REF-MAKER-01`, `REF-SJX-01`, and `REF-SJX-05` in `assets/source-manifest/finishing-references.json`. Existing URLs, hashes and rights records are unchanged. The maker animation was not treated as photographic evidence.

**Observed:** the rose/gilt plate has dense, fine, irregular relief; bright grains concentrate under illumination. At movement scale it becomes a coherent metallic field. Steel mounting pads have quieter granulation, distinct from the directional bridge brushing and smooth polished rims. Macro texture resembles overlapping irregular pits and short ridges, without a regular grid or sparse glitter. Photographic focus, exposure and reflections affect the apparent grain contrast.

**Interpretation:** a shallow isotropic relief field with a metal BRDF is a better approximation than colored noise. The photographs do not establish grain dimensions, height, numerical roughness, alloy/coating, or Marco Lang's exact frosting process. Roger W. Smith's [workshop description](https://rwsmithwatches.com/workshop) corroborates frosted/gilded plates as a watchmaking finish family, not the particular process on this watch.

The old finish was inspected in the actual browser at overview, inspection and macro scales, plus oblique views. Its broad plate showed cloudy brown mottling, especially around the lower rim; mounting pads showed pale/dark patches. Enlarging the surface enlarged the blotches without revealing fine relief. Reflection changes did not make those markings read as surface treatment. Plate and pads also used different grain scales.

## Chosen treatment and iteration

`FrostedSurface.ts` replaces that model with compact, overlapping radial impressions at independently jittered positions. Analytic slopes perturb the physically lit normal; frost does not modulate base color. An integer hash avoids float-hash precision bands and texture tiling. Coordinates are original local millimetres, without component bounds, UV seams, elapsed time, or occurrence/world-position dependence.

The initial trial covered d195's broad plate and d99's smaller pads beside polished boundaries. A first, deeper/coarser relief was rejected as too granular. A finer trial was then too filtered at ordinary inspection distance. The final balance retains fine normal detail near the camera and a quiet metallic lobe farther away. Only after these rendered trials was the treatment extended to d222 and d228.

All frosted components use the same authored scale: 24 sampling cells/mm, two irregular impressions per cell, support radii 0.5–0.85 cells (about 42–71 µm support diameter). The nominal depth coefficient is 2.5 µm per overlapping impression. These are rendering choices, not measurements of the watch. Metal colors and metalness remain authored; resolved micro-roughness starts at 0.36 and approaches approximately 0.415 when grain is unresolved.

The major axis of the pixel footprint controls smooth attenuation, including foreshortened views. Unresolved slope variance contributes to the BRDF roughness so distance does not turn frosting into polish. This Gaussian amplitude attenuation is an approximation to filtering, not exact convolution or exact GGX microgeometry integration. The independent-impression variance estimate is conservative: actual GPU sampling gave approximately 0.01150 per axis versus the 0.01293 model estimate (about 12% above the sampled field). This difference is explicit, not physical calibration.

The exact existing masks remain authoritative: d195's frosted fields and seven pads (d99 faces 28/30, d222 faces 31/33, d228 faces 49/50/52). No surface-role payload, geometry buffer, source identity, occurrence matrix, or finish-region boundary changed. The unmasked slope and footprint are evaluated before role branches; the normal is modified only after the original mask. There is no screen derivative of a masked height that could bleed into a neighboring bevel. Frost remains isotropic; satin bases, brushing, polished edges, engraving, blue components and optical finishes retain their authored paths.

## Evidence and verification

Local evidence is ignored under `artifacts/browser/frost-rebuild/`. Fixed inspection controls in `?inspect=1` reproduce overview, inspection, macro and grazing views. Eight baseline/proposal pairs cover 1440×900 and 390×844. Camera vectors match exactly, and pixel ratios, geometry counts, source poses and studio light rig match. The existing randomized contact-shading kernel is reconstructed on reload, so whole-frame bit equality is not an acceptance criterion.

Six continuous canvas recordings cover old/proposed desktop macro, phone-sized macro, and desktop inspection orbit/dolly. The camera moves through ±0.24 radians and ±22% distance. Original CAD is rendered on each frame; no in-between still frames are fabricated. Complete files decode to 179–181 frames over approximately 5.9–6.0 seconds. Encoded-frame p95 intervals are 34–35 ms (30 fps target). Earlier truncated transfers were rejected and replaced; `decoded-motion-report.json` records the completed files.

Camera-traced planar reprojection follows a gold field and steel pad in the macro clip. Broad luminance changes continuously with lighting; p95 second differences of patch-average luminance are about 1.40 and 0.89 on a 0–255 scale. Fine per-pixel variation remains: p99 second differences are approximately 32 and 31. These include video compression, resampling, trace/encoder synchronization and physical highlight changes. They are not proof of zero shimmer. Chronological decoded frames, traced patch samples and complete recordings support review of that limitation. No obvious repetitive grid, isolated glitter field, or abrupt distance switch was found in the sampled rendered sequences.

The new opt-in GPU suite evaluates the **production GLSL** on a float framebuffer. It verifies finite/deterministic relief, statistical directional balance, cell-boundary continuity, non-repetition after a 1 mm translation, monotonic minification, a finite rough lobe after residual grain vanishes, and actual screen-derivative footprint invariance under rotation/foreshortening. Seven checks pass. They test finish behavior, not arbitrary frozen constants or aesthetic quality. The CPU suite retains exact pad-face and geometry-byte checks and now protects frost-specific shader routing, source-millimetre coordinates, mask-gated normals/roughness, no color noise, and exclusion of unrelated materials.

Lint, TypeScript, production build, 10 state tests and all 84 CPU/source/runtime checks pass. The production build retains its existing large-chunk and Node deprecation warnings. Detailed browser suite totals and manual interaction coverage are recorded with this milestone in `PROGRESS.md`. The interaction suite had stale hidden-dial assumptions: it compared fitted dials to raw source matrices and expected movement-only counts. It now checks exact presentation matrices, includes enabled dial leaves in expected membership, and explicitly verifies that visibility and hand-style preferences survive navigation/Reset. Production camera, dial and pose behavior did not change. With both dials and Lance/Pear styles enabled, the interaction suite passes 32/32 at phone size and 31/32 on desktop. Desktop display-focus occupancy is 0.277 against the existing 0.28 minimum, reproduced from both starting sides; the accepted production camera and threshold were preserved. Reports retain this marginal framing result. Exact reassembly, dial preferences, reduced motion, scale, clipping, resource stability and demand rendering pass.

## Performance and remaining limits

The final effect adds no texture, mesh, geometry or draw call. Only the four definitions with frost compile its evaluator; the far-field path avoids the impression loop. A close pixel can evaluate 18 compact kernels plus filtering, so a weaker GPU could incur a material cost despite unchanged draw counts.

In matched Mac/Chromium captures at DPR 1, recorded-frame p50 was 16.7 ms; proposal p95 was 16.8 ms across desktop macro, desktop inspection and mobile-sized macro. Baselines were approximately 17.4–17.5 ms in the corresponding complete recordings. These are RAF intervals including encoding overhead, not GPU timings or evidence that the new shader is faster. They establish no material responsiveness regression in these local samples. A separate unrecorded 60-second desktop benchmark recorded 1,200 samples per phase: p95 16.8 ms assembled, 17.6 ms mechanism, and 17.1 ms separated; mean approximately 16.67 ms. The phone-sized viewport recorded p95 17.4, 17.6 and 16.9 ms respectively, also averaging approximately 16.67 ms. Results are saved in `desktop-benchmark.json` and `mobile-benchmark.json`.

Mobile evidence is desktop Chromium viewport emulation. Physical phones, other browsers/GPUs, thermal behavior and cross-device temporal stability remain unverified. Extremely close views resolve an authored relief approximation, not scanned metal. The studio rig and supplied source tessellation constrain photographic realism. The proposal is ready for the user's appearance judgment; technical checks do not constitute that acceptance, mechanical certification, or release approval.
